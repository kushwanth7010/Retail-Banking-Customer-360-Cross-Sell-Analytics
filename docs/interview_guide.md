# Complete ICICI Manager I interview study guide

**Prepare to explain the project as an independent, 100% synthetic portfolio study, NOT work done for ICICI or any real bank.** The source data and reported metrics come from `run_pipeline.py`; the financial/customer thresholds are illustrative.

## 1. Your 30-second pitch

"I developed an independent Retail Banking Customer 360 and Cross-Sell Analytics portfolio project using Python, SQL, SQLite, and Plotly, with an optional Streamlit dashboard. I generated fictitious customer, account, product and transaction datasets, built a consolidated one-row-per-customer view, and analyzed product penetration, balances and transaction activity. An explainable, consent-aware prototype produced review-only FD and credit-card discussion suggestions, with automated checks for database integrity and recommendation constraints. All data and results are synthetic; no customer was contacted or product approved."

## 2. Your 90-second walkthrough

"The business problem was that customer profile data, account data, holdings, and activity often sit in separate systems. Analysts need a consistent Customer 360 before discussing customer engagement or potential additional products. I built four reproducible synthetic CSVs: 1,500 customers, 1,500 accounts, product holdings and 79,422 transactions. I loaded them into SQLite using primary keys, foreign keys, CHECK constraints, and indexes. In SQL, I aggregated recent transaction metrics and product holdings separately using common table expressions before joining them to the customer table. This avoids duplicate counting and produced 1,500 unique Customer 360 records. I segmented customers by an illustrative snapshot balance and examined successful activity, salary inflows, existing products, opt-out and KYC flags. Then I wrote transparent rules that exclude records lacking consent or other basic checks and generate at most one FD or card discussion suggestion per customer. In the fixed-seed demonstration, 436 suggestions were produced, all explicitly marked REVIEW_REQUIRED. Finally, I created offline and Streamlit dashboards and eight automated tests. The recommendations are not credit approvals or actual product conversions; the project shows how analytics can support bank staff with explainable decision support."

## 3. The whole project in nine steps

1. **Business objective:** unify fragmented synthetic data and surface patterns that analysts can explore; no automated sales.
2. **Source datasets:** `customers.csv`, `accounts.csv`, `product_holdings.csv`, `transactions.csv` generated by a fixed-seed Python script.
3. **Database model:** independent normalized tables with PK/FK/UNIQUE/CHECK constraints in `sql/schema.sql`.
4. **ETL:** parse CSVs, bulk insert with SQLite `executemany`, reject invalid data and run `PRAGMA foreign_key_check`.
5. **Feature engineering:** three-month inflows/outflows, transaction counts, salary recurrence, latest activity and active product flags.
6. **Customer 360:** SQL CTE aggregations with one row per customer; exclude failed/returned transactions from successful cash-flow totals.
7. **Customer segments:** illustrative snapshot balance groups; monitor active vs dormant customers and product penetration.
8. **Cross-sell worklist:** explicit consent/quality gates, simple review-only FD/card rules, one product per customer and auditable rationale.
9. **Outputs and controls:** two dashboards, exported CSV reports, SQLite database, JSON KPI summary and eight automated tests.

## 4. Interview questions with model answers

**Q1. Why did you build this project?**
To practise turning multiple normalized datasets into a coherent, explainable business intelligence workflow. It combines customer analytics, SQL, Python and compliance-aware workflow design relevant to digital and retail banking.

**Q2. What exactly is Customer 360?**
A consolidated analytical view of each customer combining master information, account status, current product holdings and recent transaction KPIs. In this project the output is one row per fictional customer ID.

**Q3. Explain your data model.**
The customer table holds consent, region, synthetic KYC and onboarding flags. Accounts link to customers; transactions link to accounts; product holdings link to customers. The prototype gives each customer one savings or current account, while multiple product holdings can exist.

**Q4. What are a primary key and foreign key?**
A primary key identifies rows uniquely, e.g. `customer_id`. A foreign key enforces a valid relationship, e.g. `transactions.account_id` must refer to an account that exists in `accounts`.

**Q5. Why did you not join transactions directly to product holdings?**
If one customer has 20 transactions and 3 products, a raw join could produce 60 records and triple totals. I aggregated transactions and products in separate CTEs to the correct grain before joining the summaries.

**Q6. What is a CTE?**
A Common Table Expression is a named subquery defined using `WITH`, improving readability and supporting intermediate transformations in a SQL query.

**Q7. What is the 90-day window?**
The project uses the last three complete calendar months ending on the snapshot date: 1 July through 30 September 2026 inclusive.

**Q8. What does `COALESCE` do?**
It returns the first non-null argument. It converts missing aggregates after LEFT JOINs to zero so that customers without product holdings or recent transactions retain one complete analytical row.

**Q9. What is product penetration?**
The count of customers with an active product divided by all customers in the defined population. For example, in the synthetic dataset, 420 out of 1,500 have a credit card, or 28%.

**Q10. Why have an index on `(account_id, transaction_date)`?**
It supports efficient account-specific filtering and date-based activity queries. Actual impact would depend on query plans and database size; this project demonstrates the design principle, not a measured performance gain.

**Q11. What is the difference between inflow and revenue?**
Customer account inflows are money entering an account and do not equal the bank's own revenue. The project intentionally avoids reporting inflows as revenue, profit or fee income.

**Q12. How did you create the dataset?**
Python's seeded random generator created fictitious IDs, regions, account snapshots, product flags and nine months of synthetic transaction events. It contains no real bank information or personally identifiable customers.

**Q13. Is the average account balance reconciled to transactions?**
No. It is an independently simulated snapshot, not a ledger-derived closing balance. I document this limitation instead of claiming accounting reconciliation.

**Q14. How do you identify inactivity?**
The SQL view finds the latest successful transaction by account. An account is flagged inactive in the activity sense if the date is missing or more than 60 days before the snapshot date. This differs from the separate synthetic account-status field.

**Q15. What are your customer segments?**
Entry balance below ₹40,000, mid balance ₹40,000 to below ₹100,000, and high balance at least ₹100,000. These are illustrative thresholds, not ICICI's segmentation system.

**Q16. How does the FD rule work?**
For records passing the basic gates, it considers a synthetic savings customer with no active FD and snapshot balance at least ₹80,000. The result is only a possible topic for discussion: liquidity preferences, term and suitability require review.

**Q17. How does the credit-card rule work?**
For records passing the basic gates, it considers a synthetic savings customer without a current card, with at least two recent salary credits and average monthly salary inflow of at least ₹35,000 over three calendar months. This does not determine credit eligibility, repayment capacity or approval.

**Q18. Does priority score 85 mean an 85% conversion probability?**
No. It is a bounded sorting index based on arbitrary, documented illustrative weights. It has not been trained or calibrated on outcomes; comparing scores across product types is limited.

**Q19. Why suggest at most one product per customer?**
To demonstrate a simple, auditable prioritization and avoid creating multiple simultaneous candidate discussions. Real outreach strategy would require more comprehensive customer-specific controls and governance.

**Q20. What safeguards did you implement?**
The prototype checks simulated consent, do-not-contact, KYC status, account status, recent successful activity, returned transactions, and existing holdings. Every suggestion requires further human review. These demonstrations are not a substitute for actual bank compliance or product suitability assessment.

**Q21. Why exclude customers with a returned transaction?**
It is a conservative, illustrative operational flag to prevent the prototype from suggesting immediate outreach to those records. Returned transactions alone are not an appropriate measure of credit risk or customer suitability in a real bank.

**Q22. What is the outcome?**
The fixed-seed prototype generated 436 review-only suggestions: 221 for card discussions and 215 for FD discussions. There were no real contacts, approvals, conversions, financial gains or measurable marketing lift.

**Q23. Why SQL and Python together?**
SQL supports relational joins, aggregates, integrity constraints and reusable views. Python enables reproducible data generation, orchestration, rule logic, exports, plotting and automated tests.

**Q24. How did you test the project?**
Eight unit/integration checks confirm the 1,500-customer view, foreign-key integrity, unique recommendations, excluded ineligible records, no proposals for products already held, bounded review-only scores, expected outputs and absence of false revenue claims.

**Q25. How would you scale it in an enterprise?**
I would use approved secured data sources, entity resolution for multiple accounts, governed batch/streaming pipelines, partitioned analytical tables, role-based access controls, audit logs, monitored quality rules and human-approved policy logic. I would validate against real observed outcomes only with appropriate authorization.

**Q26. How would you measure success in a real, approved pilot?**
After obtaining authorized outcomes, monitor eligible and consented contact populations, customer experience, verified product uptake, retention and approved financial metrics, using an appropriate controlled experiment. None of those metrics are measured in this synthetic demonstration.

**Q27. What if your manager asks you to contact everyone with a high score?**
I would explain that the score is not a prediction or eligibility decision, check the actual bank's contact permissions and suitability procedures, and require authorized human review before any contact.

**Q28. How is it connected to ICICI Bank Manager I?**
It demonstrates skills in customer-oriented analysis, financial product familiarity at a conceptual level, data quality, SQL, dashboard communication, cross-functional reasoning and respect for risk/compliance. It is my independent portfolio work, not an ICICI deployment.

## 5. Practical SQL questions to rehearse

Run these queries using SQLite (`sqlite3` shell or a VS Code SQLite extension):

```sql
-- A. Number of customers in each region
SELECT region, COUNT(*) AS customers
FROM customer_360 GROUP BY region ORDER BY customers DESC;

-- B. Percentage of customers with an active credit card
SELECT ROUND(100.0 * SUM(has_credit_card) / COUNT(*),2) AS card_penetration_pct
FROM customer_360;

-- C. Potentially contactable accounts that are dormant
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM customer_360
WHERE contactable_for_review=1 AND inactive_over_60d=1;

-- D. Three customers with the most successful 90-day transactions
SELECT customer_id, successful_txn_90d
FROM customer_360 ORDER BY successful_txn_90d DESC, customer_id LIMIT 3;

-- E. Review-only opportunities by product
SELECT proposed_product,COUNT(*) AS opportunities
FROM recommendations GROUP BY proposed_product;
```

## 6. Banking/customer analytics quick revision

- **Customer 360:** unified customer view across systems.
- **Cross-sell:** discussing an additional appropriate product with an existing customer, subject to customer needs and bank policies.
- **Upsell:** moving to a different tier or expanded version of an existing product, when suitable.
- **KYC:** Know Your Customer identification and due-diligence processes; a synthetic status flag is not actual compliance.
- **Consent / do-not-contact:** permission and preference information that should be checked before marketing.
- **FD:** a deposit product with an agreed term and interest conditions.
- **Credit card:** revolving credit instrument; requires product-specific suitability, underwriting and affordability processes.
- **KPI:** Key Performance Indicator; an agreed measurable indicator with a defined denominator and time window.
- **Data leakage:** using information that would not be available when a prediction or decision is made.
- **False positive:** a suggested opportunity that turns out to be unsuitable or not useful after evaluation.
